## M11 — ch. 9 process-intensification table (Table 2)

**kLa row.** Recomputed from the table's own columns: 0.19/10⁻² = 19,
0.41/10⁻³ = 410, so **20-400×**, not 100-1000×. Fixed in all three places:
the table, the chapter opening (was "mass transfer coefficients rise by two or
three [orders]" → "one to two orders of magnitude"), and the conclusions
(was "100-1000× mass transfer enhancement" → "20-400× ... those assumed for a
conventional contactor").

I found a real source for the microfluidic side. The 0.19-0.41 s⁻¹ figure is
Dessimoz et al. 2008 (`@dessimoz2008liquid`, already in `references.bib` and
already cited elsewhere in the chapter), which measured slug-flow kLa in
rectangular glass microchannels of 269 and 400 µm equivalent diameter. Secondary
summaries of that paper quote the global slug-flow kLa as "approximately
0.2-0.5 s⁻¹", which brackets the printed 0.19-0.41; I kept the chapter's numbers
because the enhancement range the FIXPLAN specifies (20-400×) follows from them,
but the orchestrator should know the primary range may be 0.2-0.5. **I could not
read the paper itself** (Elsevier 403; CrossRef has no abstract) — this is a
web-summary verification, not a page check.

The conventional 10⁻³-10⁻² s⁻¹ column remains uncited and carries no agitation
condition. I did not delete it (it is the only baseline the chapter has) but the
new caption says so explicitly and marks 400× as an upper bound, since a
vigorously stirred contactor sits at or above the top of that range.

**Surface-to-volume row.** The chapter states its own geometry: "typically tens
of µm cross-section" (§Fundamentals and Advantages). For a square channel
S/V = 4/d, so 50 µm → ~800 cm²/cm³ and 100 µm → ~400. The printed 49-61 cm²/cm³
corresponds to d ≈ 0.65-0.8 mm, which is the capillary size range used in the
slug-flow literature the row was cited to — real, but a mini-channel, not the
chapter's stated channel. The row now gives all three values *against their
channel sizes* (~800 at 50 µm, ~400 at 100 µm, ~57 at 0.7 mm) and the caption
gives the 4/d basis and makes the point that S/V is geometric, not intrinsic to
microfluidics.

The three sites now agree:
- opening: "a 50-100 µm channel presents 400-800 cm² of interface per cm³";
- §Co-laminar: the false "surface-to-volume ratios double those in bulk
  extraction" is gone. `@kolar2016microfluidic` reports *extraction rates*
  roughly double bulk, attributed to higher interfacial area — that is what the
  sentence now says. The "2×" was an extraction-rate number misread as an S/V
  number;
- table: values quoted per channel size.

No enhancement ratio is given for the S/V row: none of the cited sources reports
a stirred-dispersion interfacial area on a comparable basis, and I was not
willing to invent a drop size and holdup to manufacture one.

**Extraction-time row.** Reconciled by making both tables use the same numbers.
Table 2 keeps 3-60 s (the residence-time range of the flow-focusing droplet work,
`@fernandezmaza2024high`) vs 10-25 min, and the ratio of extremes is **10-500×**,
not 10-100×. The earlier "Performance Metrics" table's "<10 seconds vs ~25
minutes" (the implicit 150×) was changed to "3-60 seconds vs 10-25 minutes" to
match.

**Separation-factor row: deleted.** `@dessimoz2010quantitative` is "Quantitative
criteria to define flow patterns in micro-capillaries" and
`@kashid2007hydrodynamics` is a slug-flow hydrodynamics/pressure-drop paper;
neither contains a separation factor. I found no replacement source for a generic
"2-6×" and do not believe one exists, because selectivity belongs to an element
pair, not to a contactor. The caption now says so and points to the chapter's
own paired numbers (Dy/La 279, Nd/Pr 2.72), noting that no cited microfluidic
study measured its pair in a conventional contactor for comparison. The
propagated "separation factors 2-6× higher" in the conclusions was replaced with
the paired Dy/La figure.

**Phase-ratio row** (not in the assignment, but it was the last unsourced row):
now cited to `@chen2017fast`, which demonstrably works at 200:1. The
"typically <50:1" conventional side is still uncited; flagged, not deleted.

papers; they are now cited for what they do support (flow-regime definitions).
