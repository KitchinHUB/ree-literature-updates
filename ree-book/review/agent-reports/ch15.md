## G22 — measuring D, and an oxide interference table

**New `### Measuring D`** (695 words) inserted between the D definition and the
separation-factor subsection, cross-referencing `[](#solvent-extraction-fundamentals)`
(that file has exactly one `(slug)=` label). Covers by-difference vs direct
organic analysis, matrix matching, error amplification, mass-balance closure, and
equilibration-time checks.

**One deliberate departure from the assignment text, please read.** The
assignment said "by-difference fails when D ≫ 1 (the aqueous depletion is the
small difference of two large numbers) and direct organic analysis fails when
D ≪ 1". I worked the propagation and that is inverted. With
`[REE]_org = ([REE]_feed − [REE]_aq)(V_aq/V_org)`, the depletion is *small* when
D is small, not when D is large. At D = 0.01, O/A = 1 the aqueous retains 99.0%
and the organic rides on a 1.0% depletion; 2% relative uncertainty on each of
feed and raffinate gives ≈280% on that difference. At D = 100 the aqueous
depletion is 99%, which is not a small difference at all — there the difficulty
is a different one (the raffinate is a small signal near the detection limit, and
entrained organic droplets put a floor under the apparent aqueous
concentration), and the *mirror* failure is measuring the organic and taking the
aqueous by difference.

I wrote the derivation-correct version: the general rule is **never obtain by
difference the phase holding most of the metal**, with the worked 280% number at
D = 0.01, the mirror case at D = 100 stated explicitly, the detection-limit /
entrainment limit at D ≫ 1, and a rule of thumb that by difference is defensible
while `D × (O/A) ≳ 0.3` (depletion above ~a quarter of the feed). If the
orchestrator intended the other framing, this is the paragraph to look at.
