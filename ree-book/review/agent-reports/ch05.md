## M9 — Mountain Pass and the monazite digestions

**Mountain Pass rewritten.** The old "Industrial Example" (flotation → 700 °C
roast → 70 % H₂SO₄ at 90 °C → "HCl conversion") described no real plant. It is
replaced by a new section, *Two Industrial Routes Compared: Mountain Pass and
Bayan Obo*, which sets the chloride and sulfate routes side by side:

- **Mountain Pass**: flotation to ~60 % REO concentrate → dilute-HCl pre-leach
  to strip carbonate gangue → oxidative roast (~600 °C, Ce(III) → Ce(IV)) →
  HCl leach, with Ce(IV) reporting to the residue as a cerium concentrate →
  Fe removal → straight into the chloride SX circuit, no medium conversion.
  Cited `[@gupta2004extractive; @castor2006rare]`.
- **Bayan Obo**: concentrated-H₂SO₄ roast at 400-600 °C → water leach →
  double-sulfate/hydroxide precipitation → redissolution in HCl if a chloride
  feed is wanted. Cited `[@kim2025rare]`.

I added an explicit paragraph saying that "evaporate H₂SO₄ out of an HCl
circuit" is impossible (H₂SO₄ bp 337 °C — the HCl leaves first) and that
sulfate→chloride conversion goes through hydroxide/carbonate/oxalate
precipitation and redissolution. This was worth stating rather than silently
deleting, because it is a recurring error in secondary sources. The stale
"Conversion to chloride (add HCl, evaporate H₂SO₄)" line in the flowsheet is
also gone, and the flowsheet is now the chloride route throughout.

**Monazite NaOH digestion.** The block said "Step 1: Alkaline digestion
(300-400 °C)" and then "Temperature: 140-200 °C (autogenous pressure)" fourteen
lines later. Both are now **140-150 °C in 60-70 wt% NaOH at atmospheric
pressure**, stated once, with a paragraph explaining *why* atmospheric pressure
works (boiling-point elevation of concentrated caustic) — that is the whole
economic argument for the caustic route and the chapter never made it. I added a
sentence saying 300-400 °C belongs to caustic *fusion*, a different procedure,
so the number is disowned rather than just deleted. Also added the ~45 μm
particle-size requirement (the reaction is topochemical).

**Direct HCl digestion of monazite: deleted.** The 20-line block (6-12 M HCl,
140-180 °C, autoclave) was uncited and describes a route that does not work.
Replaced with a short section, *Why There Is No Direct Hydrochloric Acid Route*,
explaining that monazite is a dense stable orthophosphate with no protonatable
leaving group, that HCl azeotropes at 110 °C so it cannot be pushed to
digestion temperature, and that every industrial flowsheet reaches a chloride
liquor indirectly via hydroxide precipitation and redissolution. This is
qualitative and carries no numbers.

**Alamine 336.** Fixed in both places. `:594` "Primary amine extraction (from
HCl medium) — Alamine 336 (trioctylamine)" is now an *amine extraction from
sulfate medium* block cited to `[@amaral2010thorium]`, whose title confirms
monazite **sulfuric acid** liquor. I state that the separation works because
Th(IV)/U(VI) form anionic sulfato complexes and REE(III) does not, that the
industrially used extractant is a **primary** amine (Primene JM-T), and that
Alamine 336 is a **tertiary** tri-C₈/C₁₀-alkylamine. The second occurrence
(Thorium Removal) now reads "Primary amine (Primene JM-T) from sulfate liquor,
or TBP from nitrate".

## M12 — the waste / ore-requirement table

Deleted the table and the `@jha2016hydrometallurgical` attribution (I could not
confirm any of those numbers are his, and the ion-adsorption row was wrong by
more than 10×). Replaced with a computed treatment that states its basis
explicitly and shows the arithmetic.

Everything is derived from grades and recoveries stated elsewhere **in this
chapter**, using `feed = 1 / (grade × recovery)`:

| Ore type | Basis | Grade | Recovery | Feed per t REO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bastnasite | **concentrate** | 60-75 % | 90-98 % | 1.4-1.9 t |
| Monazite | **concentrate** | 50-70 % | 85-95 % | 1.5-2.4 t |
| Ion-adsorption | **ore** | 0.05-0.3 % | 80-95 % | **350-2,500 t** |

with the two bracketing calculations written out in the prose
(1/(0.60×0.90) = 1.85; 1/(0.003×0.95) = 351; 1/(0.0005×0.80) = 2,500).

The basis mix is now explained rather than hidden: bastnasite and monazite are
beneficiated so the leach feed is a concentrate; ion-adsorption clay has no
mineral to concentrate so the ore *is* the leach feed. Ore behind the bastnasite
concentrate is computed separately from the chapter's own flotation numbers
(10-30 % REO feed at 70-90 % recovery → 4-16 t ore/t REO). For monazite I say
explicitly that an ore basis is **not well posed**, because placers are mined
for ilmenite/rutile/zircon and monazite is a byproduct, so any ore tonnage is an
allocation choice.

Reagents are now stoichiometric where possible:

- Bastnasite: REE₂O₃ + 3 H₂SO₄ → 3×98/328 = **0.90 t H₂SO₄/t REO** as a floor.
- Monazite: the chapter's own 1.5-2 : 1 acid-to-concentrate ratio on 1.5-2.4 t
  → **2.3-4.8 t H₂SO₄/t REO**.
- Ion-adsorption: 1.5 mol (NH₄)₂SO₄ per mol REE, 6.1 kmol REE/t REO →
  **1.2 t (NH₄)₂SO₄ minimum**. The old table's "0.5 t salt" was *below the
  stoichiometric floor*, which I say in the text.

Solution volumes: 4-12 m³/t REO for the acid routes (S/L 1:3-1:5 on the
concentrate); **1,000-5,000 m³/t REO** for ion-adsorption, from the chapter's
own 200-1,000 mg/L PLS, cross-checked against ore tonnage at L/S 1-2 m³/t
(350-5,000 m³ — the two agree). The old "100-300 m³" was out by more than 10×,
the same error as the ore row.

Solid residues: 0.4-0.9 t (bastnasite) and 0.5-1.4 t (monazite) as the
difference between concentrate in and REO dissolved. I replaced "Minimal" for
ion-adsorption with the honest statement that the solid left behind is the
entire 350-2,500 t of ore, in place or as a spent heap.

## B7 — five misattached citations in ch. 5

I verified each against the CrossRef record.

1. **`@brisson2015bioleaching`** — the abstract reports *concentrations*, not
   percentages, and its headline number is that cell-free spent medium leached
   REE to **1.7-3.8×** the concentration reached by HCl at the same pH. It also
   reports preferential REE-over-Th solubilisation. The 65-92 % / 55-75 % /
   60-80 % table is not in the paper, and *Penicillium tricolor* is not one of
   its organisms (its isolates were *A. terreus* ML3-1 and *Paecilomyces* sp.
   WE3-F). **Table deleted**; replaced with prose stating exactly what Brisson
   measured and noting explicitly that the 65-92 % figure is sometimes
   attributed to them and is not theirs. Also deleted the "REE extraction from
   monazite: 65-85 %" bullet in the *A. niger* block.
2. **`@wang2025rare`** — the abstract is a *mechanistic* study (A. niger via
   organic-acid complexation, with strong Yb adsorption onto the mycelium;
   A. ferrooxidans via Fe-S oxidation). No extraction percentages. Deleted
   "75 % in 14 days" / "65 % in 21 days"; kept the mechanism distinction, which
   the paper does support.
3. **`@corbett2017incorporation`** — the abstract gives **12.32 mg/L** total REE
   on sterile monazite and **23.7 mg/L** on non-sterile ore after 8 days,
   against **0.65 mg/L** abiotic. "Increased leaching rate 3×; 80 % extraction
   in 14 days vs. 45 %" is fabricated. Replaced with the real numbers, and
   added the point that matters: the biotic/abiotic ratio is ~20× but the
   absolute liquor is tens of mg/L, one to two orders below an acid leach.
4. **`@stojkovic2024recovery`** — a *coal fly/bottom ash* paper using ultrasonic
   alkaline roasting (95 °C, 10 min, 3 M NaOH) then microwave acid leaching
   (174 °C, 30 min, 1:1 HCl/HNO₃), ~80 % recovery against NIST 1633c, and it
   states the process is greener but recovers *less* than a high-temperature
   roast. "Reduces leaching time 30-50 %" is not in it. Rewritten to describe
   the actual experiment and its actual trade-off.
5. **`@ni2023sustainable`** — a hydrophobic-DES paper for **thorium** from
   radioactive waste leachate. "REE extraction from waste: 60-80 %" deleted;
   the DES bullet now says what the paper actually demonstrated.

Sixth, related: **`@xiao2015leaching`** is an MgSO₄ paper and was cited three
times for the *traditional* (NH₄)₂SO₄ process and for deposit mineralogy. Those
three cites are moved to `@shi2022column` (column leaching at low ammonium
concentration) and `@long2019kinetics`; `@xiao2015leaching` is now cited in the
magnesium-sulfate block where it belongs. I also replaced the "80-90 % vs
90-95 %" efficiency comparison there with the qualitative reason (Mg²⁺ is a
divalent, weaker competitor for the exchange sites).
