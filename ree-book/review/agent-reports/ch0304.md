## G19 — quantitative core of ch. 3

Built on the revised chapter; the +3 slope, `E = D(O/A)` and `E/(1+E)` were left
exactly as they were and are now the inputs to the new material. Four additions
and two repairs:

- **`#### How Many Stages for Recovery? The Kremser Equation`.** Standard form
  and the inverted stage-count solution, with notation stated explicitly
  (`x_in`, `x_out`, `y_in`, `m = D`, `E = D(O/A)`). Checked against three limits:
  `N = 1` reduces to `x_out/x_in = 1/(1+E)`, which is the chapter's own
  single-stage result; `E = 1` degenerates to the arithmetic `N + 1` (so 99.9 %
  recovery would need 999 stages, which is why nobody operates there); `E >> 1`
  gives `N ≈ ln(x_in/x_out)/ln E`. Worked example uses the chapter's own `D = 10`
  at `O/A = 1/3`: `E = 3.33`, one stage extracts 77 %, and 99.9 % recovery needs
  5.4 → 6 theoretical stages.
- **`#### How Many Stages for Separation? A Fenske Bound`.**
  `N_min = ln[(x_P/(1-x_P))((1-x_R)/x_R)]/ln β`. At β = 1.5 with 99.99 % at both
  ends, `N_min = 18.42/0.405 = 45`. States plainly that real plants run more than
  `N_min` because they operate at finite reflux, split more than two components,
  and get 90-95 % stage efficiency — installed counts are two to three times
  `N_min`. Cross-references `[](#why-rare-earths-are-hard-to-separate)` and says
  that this is the derivation behind its "hundreds of stages". Sensitivity given
  both ways: 99.9 % at both ends drops it to 34; β = 3.0 drops it to 17.
- **`#### Fractional Extraction: Extract, Scrub and Strip`.** ASCII cascade
  schematic with the feed at an interior stage; REE-on-REE scrubbing presented as
  the liquid-liquid analogue of distillation reflux, and distinguished from
  impurity scrubbing, which is a different and smaller duty (with Fe³⁺ removal
  pushed upstream, `[](#from-ore-to-feed-solution)`). Xu Guangxian's countercurrent
  extraction theory `[@xu1985theory]` credited as the origin of design-point
  start-up and of the modern Chinese plant configuration `[@yan2006rare]`.
- **`#### Saponification of the Extractant`.** Why it is needed (0.2 M loading
  releases 0.6 mol H⁺/L; a slope-3 dependence collapses `D` by orders of
  magnitude; per-mixer caustic addition is a bench practice that does not scale),
  how it is done (30-50 % of HL pre-converted to the Na/NH₄/Mg salt,
  `[@banda2015separation; @xie2014critical]`), and that ammonia saponification is
  the origin of the ammonium-nitrogen effluent — cross-referenced to both
  `[](#ion-adsorption-clays)` and
  `[](#environment-techno-economics-and-life-cycle)`, with Mg/Ca and
  non-saponification alternatives `[@liao2013clean]`. Three equivalents of base
  per mole of rare earth moved is stated as the arithmetic that makes the
  effluent unavoidable.

## G20 — ch. 4's promised ion-exchange coverage

New `### Displacement Chromatography` between the `## Ion Exchange` intro and
`### Conventional Ion Exchange Resins`, ~330 words: the loaded band on
strong-acid cation resin; the EDTA/HEDTA/DTPA chelating eluent
`[@james1968displacement]`; displacement contrasted with elution; the Cu²⁺ or
Zn²⁺ retaining ion, whose EDTA complex is less stable than any lanthanide's, so
it intercepts eluent running ahead of the band and holds the front as a step
`[@powell1956basic]`; ordering by EDTA complex stability, which rises with atomic
number, so the heavy end travels at the front; why the zones self-sharpen into
adjacent pure bands `[@spedding1947separation; @belova2024chromatographic]`; and
a throughput paragraph — bed inventory sets batch size, cycles run days to weeks,
product is dilute and must be reprecipitated, eluent and retaining ion must be
recovered `[@elouardi2023progress]` — which is why the method survives on Sc, Lu,
5N-6N oxides and the historical Manhattan-era/Ames separations
`[@spedding1947separation]`. A labelled ASCII band schematic is included.

## G21 — the canonical comparison table

The old eight-row table is gone. It quoted "2-10 (per stage)" and "95-99.9 %" for
solvent extraction with no element pair attached to either; it listed molten salt
electrolysis, which is a metal-production step and not a separation; it gave
"MOF Nanotraps 270-800" and "Lanmodulin … Pilot … TRL 4-5" with no source and no
pair; and it omitted four Part II-III chapters entirely.

The replacement has one row per Part II-III chapter plus two incumbent rows, and
six columns: best reported β **with its element pair**, feed (synthetic or real,
with composition), conditions (concentration, T, pH), demonstrated scale, TRL
with a footnoted justification, and a `[](#slug)` cross-reference. Four
conventions are stated in prose above the table — no β without a pair; "not
reported" is a legitimate entry; TRL from demonstrated scale only; conditions are
the chapter's own. Ten TRL footnotes carry the justifications.

**Rows where the honest answer is "not reported":** leaching (ch. 5),
pyrometallurgy (ch. 7), and biological (ch. 11) have no lanthanide-pair
separation factor anywhere in this book, and the table says so rather than
substituting a recovery or a Ln/Ca number. Membranes have no reported rejection,
flux or selectivity value anywhere in ch. 12.

- **Flash Joule heating.** Ch. 4 had 3-4, ch. 7 has 5-6 on a "pilot" designation.
  The only support offered for pilot status is a licensing agreement and

  **4-5** and said so in `[^trl-pyro]`, explicitly naming why 5-6 is not

  it is stated in `[^trl-micro]`. Nanofiltration and ultrafiltration for REE

- **MRT / IBC.** Not carried forward. There is no MRT row and no TRL 7-8 anywhere
  in the new table; molecular recognition technology has no sourced demonstration

**TRLs at 9** are limited to solvent extraction, displacement ion exchange, ore
