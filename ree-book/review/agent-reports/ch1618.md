## What I could not verify — read this part

1. **Binnemans et al. 2013 is cited for scope, not for the <1 % number.** I
   could not retrieve the full text or an abstract (no CrossRef abstract, no OA
   copy, no Europe PMC record). The title, authors, journal and DOI are correct
   and verified, so it is cited as the standard survey of routes and reasons —
   but I deliberately did not attach the "<1 %" figure to it, and attached that
   figure to UNEP 2011, Graedel 2011 and Reck & Graedel 2012 instead, which I
   read directly.
2. **`unep2011recycling` has no DOI and is not in CrossRef.** I verified it by
   downloading the report from the International Resource Panel and reading the
   quoted sentence out of the PDF. ISBN 978-92-807-3161-3. The bib entry carries
   a note saying so. If the orchestrator's policy is DOI-or-nothing, the claim
   can rest on `graedel2011what` and `reck2012challenges` alone, which are
   peer-reviewed and DOI-verified and say the same thing.
3. **"~90 % of separation capacity" is still unsourced anywhere in this book.**
   USGS MCS 2026 does *not* state a separation-capacity share — I extracted the
   full rare earths chapter and it gives mine production only (China 270,000 t
   of 390,000 t, ~69 %). I therefore removed the bare "roughly 90 %" sentence
   from ch. 18 and replaced it with the verified 69 % mine-production figure
   plus a cross-reference to `#supply-chain-concerns`, where the 90 % claim
   lives. **This is an open item for whoever owns ch. 1 and ch. 17**: ch. 1:59
   says "~90 % of separation and processing", ch. 17:36 says ">95 %", and
   neither has a source I could find. They also contradict each other. The IEA
   Global Critical Minerals Outlook is the usual home for the ~90 % refining
   figure, but the IEA site blocks automated fetches and I would not cite a
   number I could only obtain from a search-engine summary.
4. **Lynas, Solvay, Neo, SRC and both Chinese groups publish no nameplate
   separation capacity** that I could verify. Widely repeated figures — Lynas
   Kuantan at 10,500 t/y NdPr, Neo Silmet at 2,000 t/y, Narva at 2,000 t/y
   ramping to 5,000 t/y, Baotou city at 234,000 t/y — appear only in trade press
   and analyst notes. I entered "not published" in those cells rather than
   import them, and said so explicitly in the text so the gap is legible.
5. **Iluka's commissioning date.** The table says 2027, from Iluka's own
   6 December 2024 ASX presentation. A June 2026 trade article says 2026. I used
   Iluka's own statement and did not resolve the conflict; the more recent
   primary statement would be an Iluka release I could not reach (iluka.com is
   Cloudflare-blocked to automated fetches; the ASX PDF path is not).
6. **MP's heavy separation circuit.** Search results describe a heavy circuit
   commissioning at Mountain Pass in mid-2026 sized around 3,000 t/y of
   feedstock. The FY2025 10-K commits MP to adding samarium separation but does
   not state that capacity, so it is not in the table.
7. **The "expected to begin in late 2025" fix is only partly satisfying.** RER's
   own 24 March 2026 release confirms operations commenced and gives the
   12-month duration, but does not restate the "up to 10 tons of Nd/Pr oxide"
   figure — that comes from RER's technology page (`anon2024technology`), which
   is undated. The table marks it "demonstration scale only" for that reason.
8. Coal ash grades are cross-referenced to ch. 9 and ch. 12 rather than cited
   directly, because those chapters are owned by other agents and I did not want
   to duplicate or contradict a number they may be changing.
