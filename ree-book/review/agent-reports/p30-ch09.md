## 3. The separation-factor 279 conflict — RESOLVED

**`@fernandezmaza2024high` is correct. `@zhang2019mechanistic` was the wrong
attribution and no longer carries the result.**

Evidence:

- `10.1016/j.cej.2018.09.043` (zhang2019mechanistic), abstract via Semantic
  Scholar: it is a **numerical simulation** study — "the mechanisms … are for the
  first time unveiled by using a numerical simulation model … limiting factors …
  identified through a model-based parametric analysis". No experimental
  extraction campaign, no Dy, no La. The lanthanide pair it models is
  La³⁺/Eu³⁺ ("two kinds of lanthanide ions with distinguished extraction
  kinetics"), which is exactly the claim the chapter still makes with it at
  "Microfluidic intensification exploits kinetic rather than equilibrium
  differences" — that use is correct and was left alone.
- `10.2139/ssrn.4593980` (fernandezmaza2024high). The preprint is paywalled at
  SSRN and ScienceDirect returns 403, but the version of record's abstract is
  open at the Universidad de Cantabria repository (handle 10902/32895) and
  states: "the performance in the separation of a binary Dysprosium-Lanthanum
  system has been tested, working with a dispersed aqueous phase … and a
  continuous organic phase constituted of a solution of the extractant Cyanex®
  572 in Shellsol® D70. The extraction experiments have been conducted at
  residence times between 3 and 60 s … interfacial area that varies between 61.4
  and 49.2 cm2·cm−3 … At pH 1, 90 % of dysprosium has been extracted, and almost
  complete separation of both REEs has been achieved." The separation factor of
  279 appears in the article's own highlights ("At pH 1, 90% of Dy was extracted
  and separated from La with a separation factor of 279"). Every element of the
  chapter's claim — flow-focusing, Dy/La, 3-60 s, pH 1, 90% Dy, SF 279 — belongs
  to this paper.

Sites fixed: the `### Droplet-Based Microfluidic Systems` heading and its
