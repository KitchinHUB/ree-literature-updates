# Rare Earth Element Separations

**An orientation to the separation science of the lanthanides, for people who
have just been handed a project in it.**

:::{warning} A draft, and one assembled with AI assistance
Language models were used to search the literature, summarize papers, and draft
prose for this book. That method fabricates citations, and it did so here. Every
reference was afterwards checked against an external source, and
[](#prologue-how-this-book-was-made) sets out what was found, what was removed,
and what "verified" does and does not mean. Read it before relying on anything
in these pages.
:::

## What this book is

The seventeen rare earth elements were built by nature to have as few
differences as possible. Adjacent lanthanides differ by about one percent in
ionic radius, so any chemistry that responds to that difference responds to it
weakly, and a per-stage separation factor near 1.5 needs a hundred
countercurrent stages to reach the purity a magnet alloy demands. Every
technology in this book is either an accommodation to that fact or an attempt
to get around it.

The book began as a folder of internal literature reviews, written at different
times for different purposes, overlapping in places and contradicting each other
in others. It is that folder reorganized so it can be read in order: twenty-five
chapters in five parts, drawing on 656 cited references, with ten figures that
are generated by scripts rather than drawn by hand.

It assumes chemistry and chemical engineering, and no prior acquaintance with
lanthanide chemistry, hydrometallurgy, or solvent extraction practice. It is not
a textbook and it is not comprehensive: it reflects what one research group
read, which was driven by what that group was working on. It is also not a
source. It is a map to one.

## Where to start

[](#preface) says who the book is for and how it is organized.
[](#prologue-how-this-book-was-made) says how it was made and how far its
references can be trusted. Then **Part I**, in order — it is the only part
whose chapters assume you have read the one before.

Readers who already know one corner of the field can go straight to the chapter
they want. The Part III chapters in particular are independent of one another
and can be taken in any order, and where a chapter rests on something developed
earlier it links back to it.
